OnePass

Measurement-grade 3D from a single drone flight

In plain terms: fly a drone over an area once, and OnePass turns that one video into an accurate, map-ready 3D model. No return trips, no markers placed on the ground first.

WHAT IT DOES

  • Builds a 3D model of terrain, buildings, roads and vegetation from one ordinary flight video
  • Produces three tiers from that one flight: a fast rough preview, a realistic 3D scene, and a survey-accurate model
  • Places the model at its real-world coordinates using the drone's own GPS and altitude sensor

WHY THIS MATTERS

  • Standard drone mapping needs several overlapping flights and physical ground markers placed beforehand (ref. 10)
  • In a disaster zone or restricted area, teams usually get one flight and no time to place markers
BUILT SO FAR TIER 1 RUNNING — photos or video + telemetry → GPS-aligned coloured point cloud in ~30 s, fully offline, with per-point confidence and a browser viewer. Tiers 2–3 and measurement tools are not built. Details on slide 3.
A single pass cannot observe every surface. We separate measured geometry from inferred geometry and let the operator see which is which.

TECHNICAL APPROACH

Methodology and process for implementation (Flow Charts/Images/working prototype)

NEURAL TRACK — IMPLEMENTED, RUNS END TO END BUILT

1
Ingest selection.py

ffmpeg decode at a fixed fps; EXIF GPS, or DJI SRT matched by timestamp (≤ 0.5 s gap, no interpolation).

2
Select selection.py

Rejects blur (< 25% of the set's median Laplacian variance) and bad exposure, then samples evenly to the frame budget.

3
Reconstruct infer.py

MapAnything map-anything-apache @ 00f9c245, bf16 — one feed-forward pass over all views gives poses and depth. No bundle adjustment.

4
Georeference georef.py

Umeyama similarity + RANSAC, camera centres to GPS in local ENU. Refuses near-collinear tracks (s₂/s₁ < 0.1).

5
Fuse and export fuse.py

Voxel downsample; binary PLY carrying per-point cross-view agreement, confidence and view id.

6
Serve server.py

FastAPI worker, one cancellable subprocess per job; three.js viewer; hashed run manifests.

MEASURED — A100 80GB, 18 aerial photos: 1,134,767 points in 29.8 s (18.7 s model load, 5.9 s reconstruct), 11.1 GB peak GPU. Agreement with the photos' own GPS 1.43 m RMSE. Offline run verified in a network-less namespace.
Working prototype: a stored run in the browser viewer — video + SRT, 18 frames, 1,025,708 points, camera poses in gold. Live: bhuvan171.github.io/onepass-neural (ref. 15).
PLANNED — NOT IMPLEMENTED
  • Parallax-aware keyframe selection (current selector is interval-based)
  • Same frames through the COLMAP track; report inter-method agreement
  • Tier 2–3: dense MVS / Gaussian splatting, Poisson mesh
  • Moving-object masking; LAS/GLB/OBJ/GeoTIFF export; measurement tools
  • Independent accuracy evaluation against surveyed checkpoints
Fully offline and on-premise — no cloud service, no external API. Weights are the Apache-2.0 MapAnything checkpoint; the CC-BY-NC checkpoint is never downloaded. Two dependencies (plyfile GPL-3.0, system FFmpeg) are still under licence review.

FEASIBILITY AND VIABILITY

Analysis of the feasibility of the idea
MEASURED ON OUR OWN SYSTEM — 18-photo aerial sample, one A100 (ref. 15)
What we measuredResultWhat it does not show
End-to-end reconstruction1.13 M coloured points in 29.8 s (5.9 s inference) One sample, and a photo set — not a full-length flight video
Agreement with the photos' own GPS1.43 m RMSE; 1.62 m leave-one-out Agreement with input GPS, not ground accuracy. No surveyed checkpoints exist for this sample
Learned metric scale vs GPS3.96× too small; GPS alignment corrects it Confirms the model's own scale cannot be trusted on aerial scenes
Degenerate input handlingSingle flight line refused (s₂/s₁ = 0.009) Refusal is the correct result, not a reconstruction

NEXT GATE

  • A real drone video with its SRT through the upload path (only a synthetic video has been tested)
  • Same frames through the COLMAP track — inter-method agreement
  • Independent accuracy on a benchmark with ground truth (UseGeo, ref. 1–3)
Published work reports newer feed-forward methods leaving ~19% fewer blank patches than the standard method (ref. 1); that study did not use GPS. We have not reproduced it on our system.

  • Disaster/flood response: 119 drone flights already mapped one hurricane's damage (ref. 12)
